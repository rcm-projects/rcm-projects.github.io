01THE INTERACTION LAYER

Even a hit
is an effect.

One shared pipeline.
More ways to interact.

Direct damage and other actor interactions use the same status-effect framework. Different gameplay systems speak a common language instead of each inventing their own rules.

Shared contractCentralized rulesExtensible effects
Why a shared pipeline?

It gives interaction logic a consistent home. Systems can request effects through a common interface, reducing the amount of custom integration needed as mechanics grow.

A shared path also makes conflicts easier to reason about. Effect ordering and overlapping effects still need explicit rules.

03THE DECISION LAYER

The right move
for this moment.

Evaluate the options.
Let context change the choice.

I used Athena AI to build utility-based enemy decisions. Actions receive scores that express how useful they are in the current situation, helping the enemy choose its next move.

Context-sensitiveModular actionsTunable priorities
Why utility AI?

I wanted priorities to shift as the fight changed. Separating action scoring from execution provides a clear boundary for extending behaviors.

Utility AI is a design choice, not a universal replacement for behavior trees. New actions still require tuning, testing, and sensible rules for when decisions can change.
